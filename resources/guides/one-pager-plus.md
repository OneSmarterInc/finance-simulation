# Flexee Financial Accounting+ · What Is It Really Worth?

The full Flexee Financial Accounting course with an evaluation stage added when you set the course up: extra quarters at the end where teams study each other's firms and bid for them. Students work in teams, and each team runs its own firm throughout.

## The situation

Teams run their firms quarter by quarter exactly as in Flexee Financial Accounting. Then every firm's published statements and notes, including the accounting policies it chose, become visible to the other teams. For each rival firm, every team receives a consultant's report with a valuation built from what that firm reported. Teams build their own valuation, decide what a 51% controlling stake is worth to them, place a sealed offer, and defend their price in a presentation to the class.

## Why it's hard

The consultant's report is competent, clearly argued and built from real published figures, and it is easy to adopt its number as your own. Teams have to decide how far to rely on it and what in the published statements and notes should change their view. Every offer is capped at what the bidding firm could actually fund, so teams also have to run their own firm well enough to afford the deal they want.

## What students take away

Students learn to value a business from its published statements and to treat the notes and accounting policies as evidence. They learn that a reported figure is the product of choices, and that a price has to be defended with reasons. When the auction settles, each firm's true worth is shown beside the price paid, and that comparison is where the course comes together.

## Running it

Add the evaluation stage when you set up the course, and schedule enough quarters to include it. When the evaluation window opens, teams see each rival's statements and consultant report and save their sealed offers. You settle the auction: the highest positive offer wins and pays its own bid, an exact tie goes to the earliest offer, and you can override a tie. The reveal then shows each team its result, and the presentations and your own judgment carry the assessment. It fits an advanced financial accounting, financial statement analysis or valuation course. How many of your students would pay the consultant's number without asking where it came from?
