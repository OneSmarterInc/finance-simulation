# Flexee Financial Accounting · What Do the Numbers Really Say?

Runs across the term on the quarter schedule you set. Students work in teams, and each team runs one manufacturing firm.

## The situation

Each team inherits a manufacturing firm that makes two products and has already run for three quarters, so students begin with three full sets of statements to read before they decide anything. Each quarter they make a short set of operating decisions about how much to make, what to charge and where to buy, and they choose how the firm reports: depreciation, bad debts, warranties and similar methods. The operating side is kept simple on purpose. Its job is to give every team a fresh and different set of financial statements each quarter, and the course is about reading them.

## Why it's hard

Every quarter brings a new income statement, balance sheet, cash flow statement and notes, and the four rarely tell the same story. A firm can report a healthy profit while its cash drains into unsold stock, and a choice of depreciation method can move reported income without changing a dollar of cash. Students have to explain what changed and why from the statements themselves, and changing an accounting method takes a written justification and your approval.

## What students take away

Students learn to read the four statements as one connected system, to trace any number back to what caused it, and to tell the difference between what a firm earned and how it chose to report it. They leave able to pick up a set of accounts and say what happened, which is the skill an accountant, analyst or manager actually uses.

## Running it

Set the course up in the wizard: the quarter schedule, which decision areas open in which quarter, and whether students control their own accounting methods. Quarter 4 starts with forecasting, sourcing and production, and an area stays open once you open it. Create the firms and assign students, and each firm's warm-up history is generated for it. Each quarter closes on its own at the deadline you set and the next one opens, and quarter results show each firm's statements and flag unusual outcomes. Method-change requests come to you to approve or reject. It fits an intermediate financial accounting course or an MBA course in financial reporting and analysis. How many of your students could explain, from the statements alone, why profit rose while cash fell?
